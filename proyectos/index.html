TRABAJO FIN DE MÁSTER · UNIVERSIDAD DE LA LAGUNA

Vulnerabilidad territorial ante incendios forestales en Tenerife

Índice municipal de riesgo-vulnerabilidad y cartografía de prioridades preventivas.

QGIS / SIGAHPPCACLUSTER ANALYSIS
31municipios
10indicadores
2 kmmalla local

PREGUNTA DE INVESTIGACIÓN

¿Cómo identificar los municipios más vulnerables a incendios forestales combinando factores físicos, humanos y sociales?

El estudio construye un índice sintético para los 31 municipios de Tenerife. Integra diez indicadores agrupados en amenaza, exposición y vulnerabilidad social-territorial. La finalidad es apoyar la comparación territorial y orientar la revisión de prioridades preventivas.

RESULTADOS SELECCIONADOS

Municipios con mayor IRV

FIG / 01

Valores extraídos de la tabla de resultados del TFM. Índice relativo, no probabilidad de incendio.

METODOLOGÍA

Cómo lo abordé

  1. Reunión y tratamiento SIG de fuentes oficiales: ZARI, combustible forestal, pendientes, población, edificación, renta y factores sociodemográficos.
  2. Normalización min–max de los indicadores y construcción de subíndices con pesos 40 % amenaza, 30 % exposición y 30 % vulnerabilidad.
  3. Comprobación de robustez mediante pesos iguales, análisis de componentes principales y clasificación clúster jerárquica de Ward.
  4. Creación de un indicador complementario de prioridad preventiva (IPP) sobre una malla de 2 km.

HALLAZGOS

Qué aporta el análisis

  • Buenavista del Norte alcanza el valor más alto del índice relativo (IRV = 0,631), seguida por Santa Cruz de Tenerife (0,548).
  • El PCA muestra que los dos primeros componentes explican conjuntamente el 52,06 % de la variabilidad de los indicadores.
  • El índice diferencia perfiles territoriales, no solo un ranking, y propone la revisión de áreas prioritarias dentro de cada municipio.
HERRAMIENTAS / MÉTODOSÍndice sintético · Normalización min–max · Análisis multicriterio · ACP · Clúster Ward · QGIS
Interpretación y límites

El IRV es comparativo: no predice la ocurrencia de incendios ni modeliza su propagación. El IPP es una propuesta académica de orientación preventiva, no cartografía operativa.

Memoria académica de elaboración propia. Fuentes descritas en el trabajo: IDECanarias/GRAFCAN, MITECO, CNIG, ISTAC y otras estadísticas oficiales.

01 GEO / RISK 2026

MASTER'S THESIS · UNIVERSITY OF LA LAGUNA

Territorial vulnerability to wildfires in Tenerife

Municipal risk-vulnerability index and spatial prioritisation of preventive action.

QGIS / SIGAHPPCACLUSTER ANALYSIS
31municipalities
10indicators
2 kmlocal grid

RESEARCH QUESTION

Which municipalities are most vulnerable when physical wildfire hazard, exposure and social conditions are assessed together?

This study develops a composite index for Tenerife's 31 municipalities using ten indicators spanning hazard, exposure and socio-territorial vulnerability. It aims to support territorial comparison and the prioritisation of preventive review.

SELECTED RESULTS

Municipalities with the highest IRV

FIG / 01

Values transcribed from the thesis results table. A relative index, not a wildfire probability.

WORKFLOW

How I approached it

  1. Assembled official GIS datasets on designated high-risk areas, forest fuel, slopes, population, buildings, income and demographic factors.
  2. Standardised indicators using min–max scaling and aggregated the three dimensions with weights of 40% hazard, 30% exposure and 30% vulnerability.
  3. Tested sensitivity with equal weights, explored principal components and applied Ward's hierarchical clustering.
  4. Developed a supplementary prevention-priority indicator (IPP) on a 2 km spatial grid.

FINDINGS

What the analysis shows

  • Buenavista del Norte ranks first on the relative index (IRV = 0.631), followed by Santa Cruz de Tenerife (0.548).
  • The first two principal components explain 52.06% of the variation in the original indicators.
  • The analysis identifies different municipal profiles and supports a more localised review of preventive priorities.
TOOLS / METHODSComposite index · Min–max scaling · Multi-criteria analysis · PCA · Ward clustering · QGIS
Interpretation & limitations

The IRV compares municipalities; it does not forecast wildfire ignition or spread. The IPP is an academic prevention-planning aid, not an operational emergency map.

Original academic study. Datasets documented in the thesis include IDECanarias/GRAFCAN, MITECO, CNIG, ISTAC and other official statistics.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How can coastal flood diagnostics be translated into spatial proposals for protecting local communities?

The project analyses the vulnerability of San Andrés, Santa Cruz de Tenerife, to coastal flooding. It combines PIMA-based scenario mapping, exposed urban elements and an academic proposal for risk zoning and emergency planning.

WORKFLOW

¿Qué hábitos y barreras se relacionan con la predisposición a compartir coche como acompañante?

APPLIED DATA ANALYSIS PROJECT · UNIVERSITY OF LA LAGUNA

What shapes carpooling propensity at the University of La Laguna?

Survey data analysis and ordinal logistic regression among administrative and service staff.

PYTHONSTATSMODELSORDINAL LOGITSURVEY ANALYSIS
298initial records
228complete cases
51,75 %low propensity

RESEARCH QUESTION

Which travel habits and perceived barriers are associated with willingness to carpool as a passenger?

The analysis combines two survey datasets by participant ID, selects university administrative and service staff, and cleans records and model variables. The final ordinal logistic model uses 228 complete cases.

SELECTED RESULTS

Observed propensity distribution

FIG / 03

Percentages from the project report's final analysis sample (n = 228). Labels refer to the cleaned three-level outcome.

WORKFLOW

How I approached it

  1. Merged and cleaned survey files in Python, filtering to university administrative and service staff.
  2. Explored the response distribution by campus, gender, income and mobility habits.
  3. Estimated an ordinal logistic model using OrderedModel, selected explanatory variables and specification checks.
  4. Interpreted odds ratios, predicted probabilities and perceived organisational and autonomy barriers.

FINDINGS

What the analysis shows

  • Among the 228 complete cases, 51.75% fall in the lowest observed propensity category, 32.02% in the middle and 16.23% in the highest observed category.
  • Driving habits and barriers involving autonomy, timetable constraints and perceived safety are associated with lower willingness to ride as a passenger.
  • Descriptive gender differences are modest, while campus-level variation requires cautious interpretation due to varying subgroup sizes.
TOOLS / METHODSPython · pandas · statsmodels OrderedModel · Maximum likelihood · Odds ratios · Descriptive analysis
Interpretation & limitations

This is an observational analysis; associations are not causal evidence. Complete-case selection and the limited outcome categories constrain generalisability.

Academic project using ULL mobility surveys. No participant-level records or personal microdata are published.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How do extreme rainfall, land use and urban vulnerability interact to shape flood risk?

This report examines floods and landslides in San Cristóbal de La Laguna using documentary research, emergency information, climate sources and hydrological cartography. It considers hazard, exposure and vulnerability as complementary dimensions of urban risk.

WORKFLOW

How I approached it

  1. Reviewed relevant legislation and flood incidents, drawing on AEMET, CECOES 112 and water-management bodies.
  2. Examined drainage networks, catchments, built-up areas and exposed zones.
  3. Discussed RCP 4.5 and RCP 8.5 scenarios and interpreted territorial patterns of vulnerability.

FINDINGS
